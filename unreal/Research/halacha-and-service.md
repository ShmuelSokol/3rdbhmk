# Halachah, preparation, and the service day

Research date: 7 September 2026. Status: researched design dossier; not rabbinically approved and not an implemented rules engine. This is an educational simulation about fictional avatars, not practical guidance for entering the present-day Temple Mount. It does not infer the player's religion, lineage, medical condition, or private history.

## Evidence contract

- **P — Prophetic explicit:** something stated in Yechezkel; its application and geometry may still require interpretation.
- **H — Earlier Temple / halachic source:** Mishnah or Rambam, with the precise passage named. This is evidence for the described institution, not automatic proof of every detail of the future Temple.
- **R — Review / disputed:** competing readings, missing facts, or an unresolved mapping to this reconstruction. No automatic permission is generated.
- **D — Design:** fictional story, interface, pacing, personality, and population choreography. Never displayed as revealed detail.

Every shipped rule needs a source ID, evidence class, selected interpretation, reviewer status, affected action and zone, prerequisites, exceptions, and meaningful tests. Keep permission to enter, permission to perform a service, and permission to eat a particular food separate. Unknown is an explicit result, not equivalent to permitted.

## Preparation is more than visiting a mikvah

**H:** Corpse impurity has a distinct purification process involving purification water, the third and seventh days, immersion, and completion of the day. A bath alone is not a universal reset. [Bamidbar 19:11–12, 19](https://www.sefaria.org/Numbers.19.11-19).

**H:** Rambam distinguishes exclusions from the Mount, cheil, Women's Court, and Israelite Court. Corpse impurity, a tevul yom (immersed but awaiting completion of the day), and a mechusar kippurim (still awaiting the required offering) are not interchangeable. His account permits some states in an outer zone that it excludes from a deeper one. [Biat HaMikdash 3:3–7, 14](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/1008244/jewish/Biat-Hamikdash-Chapter-3.htm).

**H:** Kelim separately restricts the priestly court by the Israelite visitor's necessary sacrificial task, and the Holy of Holies by the Kohen Gadol's Yom Kippur service. Location and purpose matter after preparation. [Kelim 1:8–9](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Kelim.1.8-9).

**H:** Yoma describes immersion before entering for service even for someone already pure. It must not be implemented as removing an existing corpse-impurity state. [Yoma 3:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Yoma.3.3).

**D:** Represent an avatar using independent fields: fictional role and service qualification; known impurity requirements; required purification stages completed; immersion purpose and validity; day-completion status; required offering status; service-day assignment; hands-and-feet sanctification; garment state; intended task; destination zone; calendar profile; and unresolved questions. A favorable value in one field never overwrites another. The first ordinary-pilgrim scenario can begin with an explicitly authored, previously completed purification history, then teach preparation without simulating private bodily circumstances.

**R:** Do not label every court in the Yechezkel mesh using a Second Temple name just because it looks similar. A reviewer must map sacred boundaries, chamber thresholds, permitted tasks, and gate routes to the measured reconstruction. Retain the distinction between tutorial exploration and a role-bound simulation. A free camera is an educational viewing mode, never evidence of ritual entry permission.

## Modest mikvah sequence

**D:** The player approaches an opaque entrance, reads a brief fictional-avatar preparation card, chooses to proceed, and sees a neutral fade or the exterior of a closed door. Show an elapsed-time message and then a fully dressed avatar in the exit area. No nude body, wet-clothing silhouette, underwater body view, changing animation, voyeuristic camera, or intimate sound is needed. Offer immediate skip with the same learning result. This preserves the meaning without making undressing an interaction.

The interface says which modeled step was completed and which prerequisites remain. It never displays “fully pure” merely because this scene ran. No personal questionnaire is collected. Stage validity and chronology are authored scenario facts pending review, not a real-world mikvah checklist. **H:** A privacy screen is described in the Kohen Gadol's immersion account; our offscreen treatment is a design choice rather than a claim to reproduce that scene exactly. [Yoma 3:4](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Yoma.3.4).

## A calendar, not one repeating crowd loop

**H:** Early preparation and the ash-removal lottery belong to the service day; Tamid explicitly gives a variable arrival time for the supervisor. An observer checks daylight before slaughter. These are event cues, not a modern 06:00 appointment. [Tamid 1:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Tamid.1.2), [3:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Tamid.3.2).

**H:** Rambam distinguishes ash removal on ordinary days, pilgrimage festivals, and Yom Kippur, with earlier preparation on the latter occasions. [Temidin uMusafin 2:10–12](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/1013254/jewish/Temidin-uMusafim-Chapter-2.htm).

**H:** The ordinary afternoon tamid is slaughtered at 8½ seasonal hours and offered at 9½. Erev Pesach shifts those points to 7½ and 8½; Erev Pesach on Friday shifts them to 6½ and 7½. Pesach follows the afternoon tamid. These are proportional daylight hours, not 8:30 or 9:30 on a clock. [Pesachim 5:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Pesachim.5.1); [Temidin uMusafin 1:2–6](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/1013253/jewish/Temidin-uMusafim-Chapter-1.htm).

**D:** An ordinary-day scheduler uses prerequisites: preparation → assignments and daylight confirmation → morning service → eligible individual activities → afternoon service → remaining evening duties. Exact animation lengths and time compression are labeled simulation choices. Waiting can advance the fictional calendar only at an explicit transition that re-evaluates all conditions.

**H:** Shabbat has additional offerings; it is not merely an ordinary day with more visitors. [Bamidbar 28:9–10](https://www.sefaria.org/Numbers.28.9-10). Festival modules require their own offerings, songs, participation, traffic, and food rules. The Pesach account divides participants into three groups; this supports staged entry, not an invented exact future attendance figure. [Pesachim 5:5](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Pesachim.5.5). Yom Kippur must remain a distinct, separately reviewed module.

**P/R:** Yechezkel describes an east-facing inner gate's weekday closure and Shabbat/Rosh Chodesh opening, opposite-gate movement for festival visitors, and a morning offering. These must be reconciled with the chosen commentary and halachic service profile; they do not justify silently deleting another service or opening every eastern gate. [Yechezkel 46:1–3, 9, 13–15](https://www.chabad.org/library/bible_cdo/aid/16144/jewish/Chapter-46.htm).

## Kohanim are people with assignments, not permanent costumes

**P:** Yechezkel describes chambers for holy food and storing service garments, and changing before going out to the people. These chambers are not evidence that every kohen's family lives inside the sanctuary. [Yechezkel 42:13–14](https://www.chabad.org/library/bible_cdo/aid/16140/jewish/Chapter-42.htm). **H:** Middot describes elders of the serving family sleeping in the Beit HaMoked and younger kohanim bedding on the ground; that is earlier-Temple duty lodging. [Middot 1:8](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Middot.1.8).

**H:** Rambam distinguishes ordinary-priest garments, the Kohen Gadol's golden garments, and his Yom Kippur white set. He permits certain service-day wear outside the instant of service, while treating the sash differently. Do not simplify this into “every priest changes after every animation.” [Klei HaMikdash 8:1–3, 11](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/1008233/jewish/Klei-Hamikdash-Chapter-8.htm). **P/R:** Yechezkel's linen and no-wool language needs a documented reconciliation with the chosen garment interpretation. [Yechezkel 44:17–19](https://www.chabad.org/library/bible_cdo/aid/16142/jewish/Chapter-44.htm).

**D:** An NPC has a persistent assignment, next destination, appointment constraints, relationships, patience, preferred speaking style, and a short authored explanation of its immediate goal. A teacher is patient, a new kohen carefully checks his assignment, and an experienced attendant anticipates a queue. None invents halachic decisions through unrestricted dialogue. Personality may change delivery and optional social choices; it cannot change a sourced requirement. Use state and memory to create understandable intentions without claiming actual consciousness.

## Twelve gameplay cases and acceptance tests

The learning actions below are **D**, derived from the tagged evidence above. A wrong answer gives an explanation, a source link, and another attempt; it does not stage a sacrilege, humiliate the avatar, or award divine favor. Progress measures recalled distinctions and completed lessons only.

1. **Unknown preparation:** a missing fictional history field produces “review needed.” Correct action: ask the guide or choose a reviewed scenario. Test: missing data never silently becomes permission.
2. **Mikvah is not a universal reset:** an avatar with an unfinished corpse-purification scenario completes the modest scene. Correct answer: other required stages remain. Test: immersion does not clear unrelated requirements. Source: Bamidbar 19 above.
3. **Waiting matters:** an avatar has completed the relevant immersion but not the required day transition. Correct action: follow the permitted outer activity until the scenario advances. Test: the same instant cannot satisfy both immersion and later completion. Source: Biat HaMikdash 3 above.
4. **An offering can still be outstanding:** the scenario explicitly supplies mechusar-kippurim status. Correct action: identify the remaining offering process. Test: advancing sunset alone does not erase it. Source: Biat HaMikdash 3:6–7 above.
5. **Purpose matters inside:** a prepared Israelite with a reviewed sacrificial task follows its authorized route; an unrelated sightseeing request does not inherit that exception. Test: canceling the task cancels task-specific access. Source: Kelim 1:8 above.
6. **The innermost chamber is not a reward:** completing all quizzes never grants ordinary-avatar access. Test: mastery level cannot override role, day, and service conditions. Source: Kelim 1:9 above.
7. **A service-day immersion is a separate concept:** an already-pure kohen prepares for service. Correct answer distinguishes preparation from curing every impurity. Test: both the preparation flag and underlying eligibility remain checked. Source: Yoma 3:3 above.
8. **Hands and feet:** an assigned kohen remembers sanctification before service. Test: the mikvah flag cannot substitute for this distinct prerequisite. **H:** [Biat HaMikdash 5:1–2](https://www.chabad.org/torah-texts/1008246/Mishneh-Torah-Rambam/Sefer-Avodah/Biat-Hamikdash/Biat-Hamikdash-Chapter-5).
9. **Clothes match the duty:** recognize ordinary, high-priest, and Yom Kippur service sets; follow the reviewed change/storage workflow before the NPC leaves. Test: costume selection uses role and current service, not random appearance. Sources: Klei HaMikdash 8 and Yechezkel 42 above.
10. **Wait for assignment:** a kohen who has already offered incense does not automatically enter the new-to-incense lottery. Test: persisted duty history affects eligible participants. **H:** [Tamid 5:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Tamid.5.2). A lost lottery is not a failure of piety.
11. **Remember which day this is:** distinguish an ordinary afternoon from Erev Pesach and Erev Pesach on Friday; arrive relative to the chosen service sequence. Test all three calendar profiles and reject modern-clock substitution. Sources: Pesachim 5:1 and Temidin 1 above.
12. **Reverence affects the journey:** identify why a shortcut through the Mount for unrelated business is inappropriate; prepare the avatar's clothing and belongings using the reviewed scenario. Test that a shorter navigation path cannot override the activity's purpose. **H:** [Beit HaBechirah 7:1–2](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/1007200/jewish/Beit-Habechirah-Chapter-7.htm). Do not conflate this shortcut with the specifically prescribed festival movement in Yechezkel 46.

## Review required before teaching as settled

1. A qualified reviewer must approve the source hierarchy and how Yechezkel, commentary, Mishnah, and Rambam are combined. Register disagreements instead of selecting whichever rule makes gameplay easiest.
2. Approve the sacred-zone geometry and every role/task exception. This dossier supplies no present-day route map or permission determination.
3. Review purity categories, chronology, parah-water assumptions, waiting transitions, offerings, and special communal-service exceptions. No blanket “all the halachos implemented” claim is justified by these twelve cases.
4. Review garment details against the selected Machon HaMikdash reference and the prophetic/halachic reconciliation. Approve off-duty and duty-lodging behavior separately from permanent family residences.
5. Approve each calendar module, astronomical definitions, order of rites, pronunciation, and any performed sacred text. Do not render a speculative future attendance number as sourced fact.
6. Review accessibility and respectful language: ritual status is not dirtiness, moral worth, or personality. Physical service qualifications must never produce contemptuous NPCs or score penalties.
