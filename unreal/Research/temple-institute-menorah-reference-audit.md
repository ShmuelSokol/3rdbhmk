# Temple Institute manufactured menorah: reference readiness audit

Checked 2026-09-07. Scope: official Temple Institute English and Hebrew sites, including Wikimikdash linked from the Hebrew site's navigation. No third-party dimensions, downloaded images, scans, or asset changes. This supplements the [vessels dossier](vessels-garments-stone.md).

## Result

The published material identifies the actual modern object and offers useful construction/detail references. **An exact measured height, width, base footprint and branch cross-section schedule for that object were not located in this bounded official-site search.** This is a search result, not a claim that the Institute possesses no such measurements. The user-selected object can guide visual modeling, but a dimensionally exact match remains unverified.

## Actual construction versus schematic description

The [English construction history](https://templeinstitute.org/history-holy-temple-menorah/) describes the manufactured object's bronze support and gold coating. Its introductory gold quantity is 45 kg; its process account gives 43 kg and a one-millimeter coating. These published figures are not an exact geometric survey. Keep the discrepancy recorded instead of using density calculations to infer dimensions.

The [Hebrew construction response, בניית המנורה](https://www.temple.org.il/mikdashot/בניית-המנורה) independently explains that the actual Institute object began as bronze and was later electroplated with approximately 43 kg of donated gold. It points to Rabbi Yisrael Ariel's *Menorat Zahav Tahor*, chapter 41, for its practical construction account. The response discusses other possible construction methods; those alternatives should not be modeled as if they describe the existing object's interior. The book was identified but not read in this audit, so it is a promising research lead, not evidence that exact measurements have already been obtained.

The [illustrated vessels tour](https://templeinstitute.org/illustrated-tour-the-vessels/) supplies a schematic height conversion of 18 tefachim to about 1.80 m. It does not present that figure as a measured survey of the manufactured menorah. The [Wikimikdash tefach entry](https://wiki.temple.org.il/wt/טפח) explicitly records differing modern conversions, including 8 cm and 9.67 cm, and identifies its menorah illustration as a diagram explaining the division into handbreadths. Those two example conversions would produce 144 cm and 174.06 cm for 18 tefachim by arithmetic; neither result measures the modern artifact.

Production decision: retain `actualHeightCm`, `actualWidthCm` and `baseFootprintCm` as unknown. Do not promote the 1.80 m illustration estimate, a tefach conversion, the project's 0.50 m architectural amah, or a photograph's apparent scale to an exact manufactured-object measurement. A provisional silhouette can be labeled approximate while physical measurement work proceeds.

The links below are for reference inspection. Successful browser retrieval does not grant redistribution rights or establish calibrated scale.

- [Modern menorah lead photograph](https://templeinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/menora-classic-720.jpg): linked under the modern-object section of the English history page. Useful starting view; not an orthographic elevation or dimensional drawing.
- [Construction-reference collage](https://templeinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/building-the-menorah-4-720.jpg): its source-page caption distinguishes work in progress and a mock-up. Classify individual panels before borrowing geometry; a mock-up is not automatically the finished artifact.
- [Gold-application collage](https://templeinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/building-the-menorah-6-720.jpg): useful for separating bronze fabrication, supporting workshop equipment and the finished gold surface. Workshop mounting frames are not a basis for the in-world sacred object's stand.

These three image URLs were opened successfully after two initial collage fetch timeouts. No local copies were created, and this audit does not claim a complete multi-view visual inspection.

The [Wikimikdash knop entry](https://wiki.temple.org.il/wt/כפתור) explicitly attributes its photographed detail to the Institute's manufactured golden menorah and describes its ornamentation. The [flower entry](https://wiki.temple.org.il/wt/פרח) identifies the photographed upper flower as the Institute's version and locates it below the lamp, above the knop. These captions are stronger identity evidence than an uncaptioned image-search thumbnail. The flower-detail thumbnail timed out during this audit; use the accessible article as the reference locator and keep image inspection pending. The [cups entry](https://wiki.temple.org.il/wt/גביעים) supplies a useful ornament arrangement discussion, but this audit did not find an equally explicit modern-object identity statement for its pictured detail.

## Ready-to-model versus unresolved

Ready now: create an original silhouette study; name the seven lamp positions and counted ornament groups; preserve an explicit Temple Institute visual interpretation; compare recognizable surfaces against the identified official photographs; create temporary task sockets independently of final measured dimensions.

Before declaring a faithful actual-object model, obtain and record:

1. Overall height with the measurement datum stated: bottom of the actual base to body top, lamp top, and any separately mounted fittings.
2. Maximum width and depth; base footprint; branch and stem thicknesses at several heights; lamp bowl dimensions; mounting arrangement.
3. Measured front/side/rear views identifying the same object/version, with perspective and lens distortion accounted for.
4. Close-ups of the base, central lower ornament group, branch junctions, lamp seating and hidden/rear ornamentation.
5. A documented license if photographs or scans will be incorporated or redistributed, plus model/source attribution.

The comparison must exclude glass display housing, pedestals added for exhibition, lifting frames and construction scaffolding unless separately modeled as museum context. Keep visual confidence and dimensional confidence as separate fields. An attractive gold material and correct ornament counts do not establish an exact physical match or the future Temple's final appearance.
