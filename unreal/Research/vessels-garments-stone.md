# Vessels, garments, and living Jerusalem stone

Research checked 2026-09-07. This is a production dossier, not an implemented asset set or a claim to know the exact appearance of the future Beis HaMikdash. The user-selected visual reference is the Temple Institute's manufactured objects. Measured Yechezkel architecture remains the project's architectural authority. Neither historical archaeology nor a museum reconstruction overrides it.

## Menorah: object identity before modeling

Use the Institute's **actual modern golden menorah** as the selected visual interpretation. Its official history identifies the displayed object and explicitly distinguishes construction photographs from explanatory paintings. It describes a bronze support with an electroplated gold surface, not a half-ton mass of solid gold. The page gives 45 kg of gold in its introduction and 43 kg in its manufacturing account; preserve that discrepancy rather than choosing a supposedly exact weight. Rounded and straight branch interpretations are discussed separately on the same page. Do not silently combine a straight-branch diagram, the modern object's base, and a different historical relief. [Temple Institute: history and construction](https://templeinstitute.org/history-holy-temple-menorah/).

The official page's lead modern-object photograph is [menora-classic-720.jpg](https://templeinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/menora-classic-720.jpg). This is a reference link, not a licensed project texture. Exact silhouette, base, lamp attachment, ornament placement, front/back and hidden surfaces still require a documented multi-view art inspection; this text research does not substitute for that inspection.

Authoritative count checks: seven lamps; 22 cups, 11 knobs, nine flowers; height 18 tefachim. The ornament total is 42. Rambam describes the central and branch distribution and height segments, and distinguishes the requirements for gold from other metals. [Rambam, Hilchot Beit HaBechirah 3:1–10](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/1007196/jewish/Beit-Habechirah-Chapter-3.htm).

**Scale gate:** the Institute's illustrated tour gives 18 handbreadths as approximately 1.80 m; that is a schematic conversion, not a verified tape measurement of the manufactured object. The current reconstruction's 0.50 m amah would produce 1.50 m if this vessel used a six-tefach amah. The user selected the actual Institute object: prioritize its verified physical dimensions when obtained, and do not force the architecture's amah onto it. Exact physical height and width remain pending. Store original-unit dimensions, the object's metric measurements, authority and modeling scale separately. Do not rescale the building. The vessel's north–south versus east–west orientation also has an acknowledged dispute. [Temple Institute: illustrated vessels](https://templeinstitute.org/illustrated-tour-the-vessels/).

Production checklist: named countable ornaments; seven separate oil-lamp sockets and wick/flame anchors; identifiable central shaft; coherent chosen base and branches; underside and attachment inspection; gold shading under neutral and courtyard lighting; distinct display and service poses. Flame and oil states need service-schedule research, not a permanently blazing decorative candelabrum. A historical illustrated scene is not evidence that an ordinary visitor can enter the Heichal to inspect it.

## Garments: three wardrobes, task-aware changes

An ordinary kohen has four garments: ketonet, michnasayim, head covering, avnet. The Kohen Gadol's eight-piece set adds me'il, ephod, choshen and tzitz to the corresponding four. His special Yom Kippur linen set comprises tunic, breeches, turban and sash; it is a service-specific outfit, not a replacement for all garments worn throughout that day's entire sequence. Build explicit wardrobe states rather than assigning every kohen the Kohen Gadol's ornamented costume. The Institute emphasizes dignity, fit and condition. [Temple Institute: priestly garments](https://templeinstitute.org/priestly-garments/).

The Institute's second garment page is particularly useful for material families, shoulder attachments, robe hem details and the breastplate's four rows of three stones. It acknowledges that gemstone identifications are disputed. Match the manufactured choshen's documented appearance as the selected Institute interpretation; do not advertise the English mineral names as universally settled. Ordinary-priest sash composition also has variant opinions. Keep the selected sash profile explicit, and keep it distinct from the Kohen Gadol's plain linen Yom Kippur sash. [Temple Institute: garments, details and variants](https://templeinstitute.org/priestly-garments-2/).

Asset checklist: separate ordinary, golden and Yom Kippur sets; tailored body-size variants; opaque linen with woven detail; correctly attached sash, straps, settings and hems; Hebrew inscription proofread by a competent reader; no mirrored or generated pseudo-Hebrew. The model must preserve coverage through walking, bending, stairs, kneeling and camera intersections. Modest changing/immersion presentation uses an exterior transition, privacy screen and confirmation of state; no undressing or submerged-body shot. That staging is an artistic presentation decision, not a ruling that immersion alone resolves every purity condition. Bell sounds should follow actual cloth/body motion and remain specific to the appropriate garment.

## Stone: a maintained building with material depth

IAA reports establish Jerusalem limestone quarrying as a historical material analogue: the Sanhedriyya excavation distinguishes hard from underlying soft limestone, while Mount Scopus research describes soft white limestone. This supports a limestone reference family, not one universal stone color or a prediction of the Third Temple's exact quarry. [IAA: Sanhedriyya quarry](https://hadashot.iaa.org.il/report_detail_eng.aspx?id=861&mag_id=114), [IAA: Mount Scopus quarry research](https://publications.iaa.org.il/atiqot/vol63/iss1/4/).

Art direction: fresh, carefully maintained pale stone with restrained cream, ivory and warm-grey block variation. Individual blocks should differ coherently; avoid a rainbow checkerboard or noise sprayed across the whole facade. Separate block-scale tone, shallow dressed-surface irregularity and fine grain/pore response. Grain must shrink naturally with distance. Give floors, dressed exterior faces and protected interiors separate finish controls. Localized handling or foot traffic can be subtle; blanket black cracks, moss, broken edges and brown age stains would depict a ruin. Preserve source geometry, opening dimensions, stair heights and collision. Cosmetic joints remain labeled artistic wherever masonry courses are not source-measured.

Unreal implementation proposal: one parameterized stone master with bounded block variation, reusable material instances and physically scaled detail coordinates. Keep stone nonmetallic; control finish through roughness and normal detail, not emissive brightness or painted highlights. Base color must not contain baked directional shadow. Gold gets a separate metallic material. Epic documents these distinct inputs; limestone color/roughness values must be calibrated from suitable references, not invented as scientific measurements. [Epic: physically based materials](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/physically-based-materials-in-unreal-engine).

## Acceptance gates and proposed budgets

These are design targets, **not measured results or acceptance claims**:

- Start with one exterior wall, one shaded wall, one floor and one close-view vessel. Capture matched before/after viewpoints with exposure fixed and lighting settled. Reject a change that only looks better because exposure changed.
- Check stone at proposed 0.5 m, 2 m, 10 m and courtyard distance; stand still, walk, turn and traverse stairs. Require no visible repeat grid, crawling grain, normal-map seams, stretched UVs or white clipping that hides relief. Compare morning, midday and sheltered views.
- Texture trial budget: begin at 2K for repeatable stone sets, with 4K restricted to demonstrated close-view need. This is a starting allocation, not a claim that resolution guarantees realism. Report cooked disk size, resident texture memory and streaming behavior before promotion.
- Preserve original asset geometry/collision hashes where feasible. Assert vessel ornament/lamp counts, wardrobe state, inscription orientation and selected scale profile independently of screenshot beauty.
- On the RTX 2070 / 8 GB system, measure an identical packaged route before and after. Proposed overall playability target: 60 fps at 1280×720 where achievable; disclose median and slow-frame behavior. No assumed per-material GPU cost, crowd capacity or VRAM headroom. A visual change must not introduce sustained streaming warnings or new hitches.
- Promotion requires source review, rights review for incorporated assets, close and wide visual review, motion review and packaged runtime review. File counts, successful imports and a passed cook do not satisfy these gates.

Remaining research dependencies: exact manufactured-object multi-view geometry and measurements; selected shiur profile; vessel orientation policy; service-specific changing/tending schedules; reference image rights if incorporation is desired; cloth animation and Hebrew proofreading; measured performance. No source meshes, materials, runtime code or architecture were edited by this dossier.
